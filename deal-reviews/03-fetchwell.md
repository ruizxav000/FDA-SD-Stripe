# Deal review 03: Fetchwell

*Mock transcript. The customer, the people and the conversation are invented for a demo. Nothing here is a real statement by or about any company.*

- **Outcome:** Won
- **Competitor:** Braintree
- **Incumbent:** Braintree
- **Customer:** Pet-care marketplace app (owners book sitters and walkers), US, launching in Canada and the UK
- **Account executive:** Priya
- **Reviewer:** Product marketing (PMM)

---

**PMM:** Thanks, Priya. Same seven questions I ask on every review. What happened at the customer that started this evaluation?

**Priya:** Expansion. They were launching Canada, then the UK, and sitters have to get paid locally. And their payments code was, their VP of Engineering, Daniel, called it "a haunted house." Two engineers more or less full-time just keeping sitter payments working. There was also a roadmap item, faster pay for sitters, that had been pushed three quarters in a row.

**PMM:** Who made the final call, and how do you know?

**Priya:** Daniel. He signed. The CFO joined one call for ten minutes, asked one question, "does this change when sitters get paid?", and left. Daniel told me early, "this is an engineering decision, finance will sign what I bring them." And that's what happened.

**PMM:** What did the customer bring up about the competitor?

**Priya:** Two things. Their account manager had changed three times in two years. Daniel said, "I've stopped learning their names." And the PayPal piece. A lot of pet owners pay with PayPal and Venmo, and they were nervous about what happens to those buttons.

**PMM:** What changed in their questions after they'd met with them?

**Priya:** They had a call with Braintree in the middle of the evaluation. I know because the questions changed. It went from "how fast can we build this" to "what do we lose." Do we lose PayPal, do we lose Venmo, what happens to the saved cards. I assume Braintree told them they'd lose things. I don't know that. That's me guessing.

**PMM:** Which part of our pitch got a reaction, and which got silence?

**Priya:** Reaction: the docs and the sandbox. One of their engineers, on her own time, built a working sitter payment flow over a weekend and showed it at Monday standup. I didn't even know until Daniel told me. And the marketplace piece. Onboarding sitters, the identity checks, paying out in local currency.

Silence: the acceptance-rate slide. I did the whole thing, the average improvement customers see. Daniel said, "our average booking is 38 dollars, I'm not losing sleep over auth rates." And we moved on.

**PMM:** When did you first feel this was won? What happened right before?

**Priya:** The weekend prototype. Before that it was a bake-off. After that Daniel stopped saying "if."

**PMM:** What did they ask for that we couldn't show?

**Priya:** Nothing, really. It was a clean one.

**PMM:** Earlier you said they were nervous about the PayPal and Venmo buttons. What did we show them on that?

**Priya:** ...We kind of danced around it. We said they could keep offering PayPal and we'd work through the details in implementation. I didn't have a crisp answer on Venmo in the room, and I still don't. They decided it was a risk they'd take.

**PMM:** And the saved cards?

**Priya:** That one we handled. We walked them through moving saved cards over so nobody has to re-enter anything. But it was a doc and a conversation. Daniel asked, "who's done this at our size without losing cards?" and I said I'd get back to him. I didn't.

**PMM:** Did he raise it again?

**Priya:** Once, in the kickoff. His engineer said, "we'll find out."

**PMM:** What exact words did they use for their problem?

**Priya:** "Haunted house." "Payments shouldn't be a team," he said that a lot. "We want to stop babysitting this." On the sitter side it's "paid by Friday." The whole promise to sitters is paid by Friday. Nobody there says "marketplace." They say "the app." And nobody says "payouts." They say "sitter pay."

**PMM:** Anything else I should know?

**Priya:** Only that I barely sold this. Their engineer did.
