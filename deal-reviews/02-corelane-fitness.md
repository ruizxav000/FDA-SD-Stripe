# Deal review 02: Corelane Fitness

*Mock transcript. The customer, the people and the conversation are invented for a demo. Nothing here is a real statement by or about any company.*

- **Outcome:** Won
- **ACV:** $210,000 per year
- **Products:** Billing (Terminal is phase two)
- **Competitor:** Global Payments
- **Incumbent:** Global Payments (through the customer's studio management software)
- **Customer:** Boutique fitness chain, 62 studios, monthly memberships plus front-desk retail
- **Account executive:** Marcus
- **Reviewer:** Product marketing (PMM)

---

**PMM:** Appreciate you doing this the week after close. This is the Corelane Fitness review. I'll go through the same questions I ask everyone. What happened at the customer that started this evaluation?

**Marcus:** Churn. They report member churn to the board every quarter, it had ticked up two quarters running, and somebody finally split it into people who cancelled and people whose card just failed. The second bucket was a lot bigger than anyone expected. Maya, she's head of digital, called them "members who never meant to leave." Which, great line.

**PMM:** How big was that bucket?

**Marcus:** I want to say a third of the churn? Don't quote me. It was in her board deck.

**PMM:** Before that, how big was the deal?

**Marcus:** Two hundred and ten K ACV. And that's memberships only, the front desks aren't in it.

**PMM:** And what were we selling them?

**Marcus:** Billing for the memberships. Terminal for the front desks was in the proposal, but that's phase two.

**PMM:** Who made the final call, and how do you know?

**Marcus:** Maya. She ran the evaluation, she was in every meeting, she's the one who told me where we stood. She said it was her call.

**PMM:** Okay. What did the customer bring up about the competitor?

**Marcus:** Global Payments was already in there, through the studio software. What Maya told me is that they came back with a rate review and a new terminal bundle for the front desks, on a longer contract. I didn't see any of it. That's her telling me. My read is they were rattled, but that's my read, not hers.

**PMM:** What changed in their questions after they'd met with them?

**Marcus:** Hardware. Suddenly everything was hardware. Before that meeting it was all failed payments and retries. After it was, what readers do you have, do they work inside our studio software, what's the contract term, what does it cost to get out. Greg asked about termination fees twice.

**PMM:** Who's Greg?

**Marcus:** The controller.

**PMM:** Which part of our pitch got a reaction, and which got silence?

**Marcus:** The reaction was failed payment recovery. Specifically when our solutions engineer, Tobi, took an export of one month of their failed renewals and showed which ones were the kind that usually come back with a retry or an updated card. Their file, not our average. Greg hadn't said a word in two meetings, and he asked for the spreadsheet.

Silence. I opened with the global slide. They have studios in the US and a handful in Canada. And when I said "unified commerce," Maya goes, "is that the readers?" So. Yeah.

**PMM:** When did you first feel this was won? What happened right before?

**Marcus:** When Greg asked for the spreadsheet. Before that I honestly thought we were there to keep the incumbent honest. The paper had gone to Greg and it sat with him for two weeks. Nothing. Then we did the data session, and four days later it was signed.

**PMM:** So the contract was waiting on Greg.

**Marcus:** Yeah. His signature's on it.

**PMM:** Then who made the final call?

**Marcus:** ...Okay, fair. Maya chose us. Greg decided whether anybody got chosen.

**PMM:** What did they ask for that we couldn't show?

**Marcus:** The front desk. They asked to see our reader working inside their studio management software. Check a member in, sell a water bottle, same screen. We couldn't show that live. It runs through a partner integration, and the partner did a call the week after. It nearly cost us. Maya said if front-desk staff have to use two screens, "the studio managers will revolt."

**PMM:** Did they see it before they signed?

**Marcus:** They saw the partner's demo. Not in their own setup. And to be straight with you, what they signed is memberships. Online billing went first. The studios are phase two.

**PMM:** So who's processing at the front desk today?

**Marcus:** Still Global.

**PMM:** Is there a date on phase two?

**Marcus:** Not yet. It depends on the partner piece.

**PMM:** Last one. What exact words did they—

**Marcus:** Can we do that one over Slack? I've got a customer call at the top of the hour and I need five minutes to prep.

**PMM:** Sure. Go.
