# Deal review 04: Marlowe & Finch

*Mock transcript. The customer, the people and the conversation are invented for a demo. Nothing here is a real statement by or about any company.*

- **Outcome:** Lost
- **ACV:** $600,000 per year
- **Competitor:** Adyen
- **Incumbent:** Separate providers for stores and for online
- **Customer:** Apparel retailer, about 120 stores plus ecommerce
- **Account executive:** Tom
- **Reviewer:** Product marketing (PMM)

---

**PMM:** Thanks for doing this, Tom. This is the Marlowe & Finch review. I know it's not a fun one. What happened at the customer that started this evaluation?

**Tom:** Returns. Last holiday season. Buy online, return in store. The store couldn't see the online order, so staff were refunding to gift cards or telling people to mail it back. And they'd just launched a loyalty program that was supposed to work everywhere and didn't, because store and web were two different customer records. Their COO, Angela, apparently stood in a store on December 27th watching the returns queue. That's the story everybody there tells.

**PMM:** Before that, how big was the deal?

**Tom:** We proposed six hundred K a year. Stores and online together, one contract.

**PMM:** Who made the final call, and how do you know?

**Tom:** The CTO, Raj. He ran the RFP, he was my contact the whole way.

**PMM:** What did the customer bring up about the competitor?

**Tom:** This is secondhand. Raj told me afterwards. He said they came in with a retail team, people who'd done store rollouts, and they talked about one platform for the tills and the website, on one contract. And they named retailers Angela knew. That's what Raj said. I wasn't there.

**PMM:** What changed in their questions after they'd met with them?

**Tom:** Before, it was all web. Conversion, checkout, wallets. After, it was stores. What happens when the store wifi drops. How do you roll out 120 stores. Who shows up when a till goes down on a Saturday. Raj forwarded me a list of 14 questions and 11 of them were about stores.

**PMM:** Which part of our pitch got a reaction, and which got silence?

**Tom:** Reaction: the online checkout demo. Their ecommerce lead loved it. The wallets, the one-click piece.

Silence: the unified commerce part. I had the slide. Online and in-store on one platform, the line between them has pretty much disappeared. Their head of store operations, Bev, looked at it and said, "that's a diagram." Then she asked how a return works. Step by step. At the till.

**PMM:** What did you say?

**Tom:** I described it.

**PMM:** When did you first feel this was lost? What happened right before?

**Tom:** The store ops workshop. They asked for a half day on stores. I brought our solutions engineer and me. They had six people from store operations. Nobody on our side had run a store rollout. When Bev asked who does the installs, I said we'd bring in a partner. She wrote something down and didn't ask another question. I knew on the drive home.

Right before that, honestly, we were ahead. Raj said so. We'd won the web side.

**PMM:** Who asked for that workshop?

**Tom:** Angela's team. Stores.

**PMM:** And who signed with Adyen?

**Tom:** Angela.

**PMM:** So who made the final call?

**Tom:** ...Probably Angela. Raj wanted us. I think. He told me he did.

**PMM:** How many times did you meet Angela?

**Tom:** Once. The kickoff.

**PMM:** What did they ask for that we couldn't show?

**Tom:** A retailer they could call. We sent a case study. Fashion retailer, stores and online, good story. Angela's response was, "can I talk to them?" and we couldn't set up a call in time. Then a rollout plan. Dates, who installs, what happens to the old tills. And the return. They wanted to watch an online order get returned at a store till, live. We described it. We never showed it.

**PMM:** What exact words did they use for their problem?

**Tom:** "One system for the tills and the website." That was Bev, every time. Angela said, "I don't want two vendors pointing at each other." And "one customer, one basket." They never said "unified commerce." We kept saying "in-person payments" and "point of sale." They said "the tills."

**PMM:** Anything else?

**Tom:** I think we had the better product online. I still think that.
