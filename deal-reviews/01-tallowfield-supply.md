# Deal review 01: Tallowfield Supply

*Mock transcript. The customer, the people and the conversation are invented for a demo. Nothing here is a real statement by or about any company.*

- **Outcome:** Won
- **Competitor:** Fiserv
- **Incumbent:** Fiserv (through the customer's bank)
- **Customer:** Regional restaurant-supply distributor, 14 branches, launching an online ordering portal
- **Account executive:** Dana
- **Reviewer:** Product marketing (PMM)

---

**PMM:** Thanks for making time. This is a deal review, not a forecast call, so I'm not grading anything. I just want what happened. Start at the beginning. What happened at the customer that started this evaluation?

**Dana:** So, honestly, I thought it was the online ordering portal. That's what the inbound said. They sell to restaurants, kitchen equipment, disposables, all of that, and all their ordering was reps and phone calls. They were building a portal so a chef could reorder at midnight. So I went in pitching checkout.

**PMM:** And was it the portal?

**Dana:** Kind of? The portal was the excuse. The real thing, and I didn't get this until maybe the third call, was they had a new CFO. Ines. She'd been there about four months, and her first month-end close took nine days. A chunk of that was two people in finance matching deposits from the bank against the processor statements against invoices in the ERP. By hand, in spreadsheets. She said, and I wrote this down, "month-end is a week of archaeology."

**PMM:** Who made the final call, and how do you know?

**Dana:** Ines. A hundred percent. I know because the IT director, Paul, who was my day-to-day, told me straight up, "I can recommend, she decides." And she's the one who signed. She's also the one who sat on the first proposal.

**PMM:** Sat on it?

**Dana:** We sent the first proposal to Paul and it was all about the portal. The checkout, the APIs. Then nothing happened for three weeks. Turned out Paul had forwarded it to her, she read the first page and said it wasn't her problem.

**PMM:** What did the customer bring up about the competitor?

**Dana:** Fiserv was the incumbent, through their bank. I honestly don't know what Fiserv showed them, if anything. I never saw a deck. What Ines told me was that when she called the bank to say they were looking around, the answer she got was, "we can take a look at your rates." And she said, "I didn't ask about my rates." That's about all I've got.

**PMM:** What changed in their questions after they'd talked to the bank?

**Dana:** Not much. If anything she got more annoyed. She did ask us one new thing afterwards, whether she'd get a named contact. Her bank rep had changed and she didn't know who the new one was. I said yes, me. [laughs]

**PMM:** Which part of our pitch got a reaction, and which got silence?

**Dana:** Silence was the whole opening. I ran the standard first-call deck. Financial infrastructure, global growth, all the currencies. They sell to restaurants in three states. Paul was polite about it. Ines wasn't on that call, thank god. And the developer story went nowhere. Paul's team is four people and they aren't building anything. The portal is off the shelf with a plugin.

The reaction was the payout reconciliation. Our solutions engineer, Wen, showed how each payout ties back to the individual transactions and how that feeds the ERP. Ines actually stopped him and said, "go back." Then she asked if she could see it with their data.

**PMM:** When did you first feel this was won? What happened right before?

**Dana:** That meeting. Well, the one after. Wen asked for one month of their statements, the controller sent them over, and Wen and I sat with the controller and worked out how many hours the matching took. It was something like 60 hours a month across two people. Then Wen put a dollar figure on it using their own cost for those people, plus the write-offs from deposits they never managed to match. I don't remember the exact figure. Low six figures a year. Ines looked at it and said, "that's my number, not yours." Meaning it was built from her data. After that it was paperwork.

**PMM:** Before that meeting, where did you think it stood?

**Dana:** Dead, honestly. I had it at 20 percent.

**PMM:** What did they ask for that we couldn't show?

**Dana:** A reference. She wanted to talk to another distributor. Wholesale, invoices, that kind of business. We had retail stories and software stories. I sent her a retail case study and she said, "this is a company that sells sweaters." We never got her a call. She signed anyway, but she brought it up twice.

Oh, and the branches. They have 14 branches with counter sales, and she asked if the card readers at the counter would land in the same report. Wen said yes and showed a slide. We didn't demo it. She let it go.

**PMM:** What exact words did they use for their problem?

**Dana:** "A week of archaeology," that was hers. The one she said most was, "I just want to know where my money is." She never once said "payments." She'd say "the deposits." Paul would say "the processor." Nobody said "infrastructure."

**PMM:** Anything you'd do differently?

**Dana:** Find the CFO sooner. I lost a month pitching a portal.
