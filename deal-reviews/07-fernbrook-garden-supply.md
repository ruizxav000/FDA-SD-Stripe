# Deal review 07: Fernbrook Garden Supply

*Mock transcript. The customer, the people and the conversation are invented for a demo. Nothing here is a real statement by or about any company.*

- **Outcome:** Lost
- **Competitor:** Fiserv
- **Incumbent:** Fiserv (through the customer's bank)
- **Customer:** Online garden and outdoor equipment retailer (greenhouses, mowers, tools), spring peak season
- **Account executive:** Sam
- **Reviewer:** Product marketing (PMM)

---

**PMM:** Thanks, Sam. Same questions I ask on every review. What happened at the customer that started this evaluation?

**Sam:** Their ecommerce director, Lucy, came inbound. Orders failing at checkout. She said about one in twelve online orders was failing at the payment step, and it was worse on the big baskets. The greenhouses, the ride-on mowers. Customers would call in annoyed, or just not come back. And she couldn't get a straight answer from the processor on why.

**PMM:** One in twelve. Was that her number?

**Sam:** Her number. Off their own dashboard.

**PMM:** Who made the final call, and how do you know?

**Sam:** The CFO, Martin. Lucy was the champion, she wanted it, IT was fine with it. Martin said no. Lucy told me. I never met him.

**PMM:** Never?

**Sam:** We asked. Lucy said he doesn't take vendor meetings and she'd carry it.

**PMM:** What did the customer bring up about the competitor?

**Sam:** They were on Fiserv through their bank, had been forever. What Lucy told me is that when the bank heard, they came back and sharpened the rate. That's it. No new product, nothing. Just a better rate.

**PMM:** What changed in their questions after that?

**Sam:** Everything became about the rate. Up to then Lucy was asking about declines. Why big orders fail, what we'd do about it. After the bank came back, every email was the rate. Can you do better on the rate.

**PMM:** Which part of our pitch got a reaction, and which got silence?

**Sam:** Lucy loved the checkout. And the decline piece. When our solutions engineer, Farah, explained why big-ticket orders get knocked back and what we do about it, Lucy was nodding the whole way. Silence, honestly, nothing bombed. It was a good cycle. We lost on price.

**PMM:** When did you first feel this was lost? What happened right before?

**Sam:** When Lucy came out of her meeting with Martin. She called me and said, "he's not going to pay more for the same thing." Right before that she'd been telling me it was a formality.

**PMM:** You said price. What were they comparing our price to?

**Sam:** Their rate with the bank. The controller did a one-pager for Martin. Our rate, their rate, side by side.

**PMM:** Just the rate lines?

**Sam:** Yeah. The per-transaction number. Which isn't fair, because they pay a gateway fee on top, and they've got a person who does nothing but chargebacks. But that's not on the statement line, so it wasn't on the page.

**PMM:** Was anything from us on that page?

**Sam:** Our quote.

**PMM:** Who on their side saw the number, and what did they say?

**Sam:** Martin and the controller. I wasn't in the room. Lucy presented it. What he said is what I told you. "Why would I pay more for the same thing."

**PMM:** "The same thing."

**Sam:** Yeah. Which it isn't. But that's what he said.

**PMM:** Did we put a figure on the value they'd get?

**Sam:** We showed the uplift slide. The average improvement in acceptance. We talked about it a lot. Lucy got it.

**PMM:** A figure for Fernbrook.

**Sam:** Not in a doc, no. Farah offered to run their declines. She wanted a three-month export so she could show what the failed orders were worth. But Lucy would've had to get it out of IT, it was the last two weeks of the quarter, and I didn't want to slow things down. Lucy was sold. I didn't think we needed it.

**PMM:** So what did Martin have in front of him about what one in twelve orders was costing?

**Sam:** ...Nothing from us. I assume Lucy told him.

**PMM:** If we had matched the price, would we have won? What else would have needed to be true?

**Sam:** Yes. Probably. I mean, their IT lead was a bit nervous about moving before spring, that's their whole year. He'd said he'd do it if finance approved. And Martin would still have been looking at a page that said same rate, so why move. So maybe not only the rate. But the rate is what killed it.

**PMM:** What did they ask for that we couldn't show?

**Sam:** Lucy asked once if we had something she could hand Martin. Like a one-page business case. I sent her the deck and the pricing proposal.

**PMM:** Was there a business case in the deck?

**Sam:** The customer stories. The average uplift.

**PMM:** What exact words did they use for their problem?

**Sam:** Lucy said "orders dying at the last step." And "the big baskets." She said, "we do all the work to get someone to buy a four-thousand-dollar greenhouse and then the card says no." From Martin, secondhand, all I have is "the same thing."

**PMM:** Last one. If you ran it again?

**Sam:** Get more room on the rate, earlier. If we'd had more room on the rate, we win this.
