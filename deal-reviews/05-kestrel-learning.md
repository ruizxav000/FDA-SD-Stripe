# Deal review 05: Kestrel Learning

*Mock transcript. The customer, the people and the conversation are invented for a demo. Nothing here is a real statement by or about any company.*

- **Outcome:** Lost (renewal: international volume moved, US volume stayed)
- **ACV:** $320,000 per year
- **Products:** Payments (they also use Billing)
- **Competitor:** Adyen
- **Incumbent:** Stripe
- **Customer:** Online language-learning subscription with subscribers in more than 40 countries, a customer for four years
- **Account executive:** Elena
- **Reviewer:** Product marketing (PMM)

---

**PMM:** Thanks, Elena. This is the Kestrel Learning review. Same questions as every review. What happened at the customer that started this evaluation?

**Elena:** A new head of payments. Stefan. They'd never had one. Payments sat under an engineering manager, the guy who originally bought us, and he left last year. Stefan came from a much bigger company, and the first thing he did was pull declines by country. A few of their international markets looked bad to him.

**PMM:** Which ones?

**Elena:** I don't know exactly. He mentioned Brazil and a couple in Europe. I never saw his analysis.

**PMM:** Before that, how big was the deal?

**Elena:** The account was worth about three hundred and twenty K a year. Roughly half of that was international.

**PMM:** And what were we selling them?

**Elena:** Payments. They're on Billing too, but the renewal was about the payments volume.

**PMM:** Who made the final call, and how do you know?

**Elena:** Stefan recommended it and the CFO approved. Stefan told me that on our last call. He said, "this went to the CFO two weeks ago."

**PMM:** Two weeks before your last call?

**Elena:** Yeah.

**PMM:** What did the customer bring up about the competitor?

**Elena:** What Stefan told me is that Adyen ran a test on a slice of their international traffic and came back with results by country. The phrase he kept using was "local acquiring." I can't tell you what the test showed. He wouldn't share it.

**PMM:** What changed in their questions after they'd met with them?

**Elena:** They got very specific. What's our approval rate in this country, on this card type, by issuer. And why.

**PMM:** What did we answer with?

**Elena:** The Authorization Boost deck. The average uplift customers see. Adaptive Acceptance, the machine learning piece. He said, "that's an average. What's mine?" And then, I'm quoting, "I can't take an average to my CFO."

**PMM:** Which part of our pitch got a reaction, and which got silence?

**Elena:** It's a renewal, so we weren't really pitching. They like the product. The developers love us, billing works, nobody wanted to rip anything out. That's why the US stayed. Silence was the uplift number, like I said. And I offered a roadmap session and he didn't take it.

**PMM:** When did you first feel this was lost? What happened right before?

**Elena:** I don't think there was a moment. It was kind of decided before we got in the room. I'm not sure there was anything to do.

**PMM:** When did you find out they were evaluating?

**Elena:** When the renewal notice went out and Stefan replied asking to split the contract.

**PMM:** When was the last business review before that?

**Elena:** ...Before I took the account. So nine months? Ten. It was a healthy account. Growing, no tickets. It wasn't on anybody's list.

**PMM:** Had you met Stefan before the renewal?

**Elena:** No. I knew the engineering manager had left. I didn't know they'd hired a head of payments.

**PMM:** What did they ask for that we couldn't show?

**Elena:** A market-by-market view of their own approval rates, with reasons. We did get it to him. Our payments performance team did a proper analysis. But that landed three weeks after he asked, and by then the test was finished. And a person. He asked who at Stripe looks at his numbers every month, and I didn't have a name.

**PMM:** What was in the analysis when it landed?

**Elena:** Real findings. Things we could have fixed. He said it was the most useful thing we'd sent him.

**PMM:** What exact words did they use for their problem?

**Elena:** "Money left on the table." That was his headline. "Local cards, local rails." And "what's mine." He doesn't say "authorization," he says "approvals." And one thing that stuck with me. He said, "I heard from Stripe when I said I was leaving."

**PMM:** Anything else?

**Elena:** They went with local acquiring. I don't know what I'd have pitched against that.
