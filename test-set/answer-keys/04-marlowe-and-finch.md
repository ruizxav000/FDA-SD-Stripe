# 04 — Marlowe & Finch — Answer key

**Input:** `04-marlowe-and-finch.txt` (plain text). The AE is **Tom**.

## Header
- **ACV:** $600,000 per year, quoting the AE's words.
- **Outcome:** Lost · **Scope note:** none
- **Competitor:** Adyen · **Incumbent:** separate providers for stores and for online
- **Tag:** **In-store and online together**. Acceptable alternative: *Support and service* (rollout team, installs).

## Messaging changes: expected direction
1. **Replace diagram claims with a demonstrated store workflow.** "Online and in-store on one platform, the line between them has pretty much disappeared" got *"that's a diagram."* Show an online order being returned at a store till, live.
2. **Use their language:** "the tills," "one customer, one basket," "one system for the tills and the website." Not "unified commerce," "in-person payments" or "point of sale."
3. **Add rollout capability to the story:** dates, who installs, what happens to the old tills. "We'd bring in a partner" ended the conversation.
4. **Proof to add:** a retailer reference they can call, and a rollout plan.

## Sections

**Trigger**
- Evidence: holiday buy-online-return-in-store failures (stores couldn't see online orders, refunds went to gift cards). A loyalty program broken by separate store and web customer records. The COO, Angela, watching the returns queue on December 27 (fact, secondhand: "the story everybody there tells").
- Insight: the trigger was an **operational store problem**, owned by the COO.

**Who decided**
- Evidence: the rep first says Raj (CTO). Angela signed with Adyen (fact). The rep: *"Probably Angela. Raj wanted us. I think."* He met Angela once, at kickoff (fact). Angela's team requested the store workshop (fact).
- Insight: **contradiction. We sold to the CTO and won the web side. The decision sat with the COO, who owned stores.**

**What the customer told us about the competitor**
- Evidence: relayed by Raj afterward: Adyen came with a retail team that had done store rollouts, pitched "one platform for the tills and the website, on one contract," and named retailers Angela knew (fact, reported by the customer; the rep: "I wasn't there"). Afterward, the questions shifted from web to stores. **11 of Raj's 14 forwarded questions were about stores** (fact).
- Insight: the competitor sold the store rollout and the people. The question shift was visible weeks before the loss.

**Our message**
- Evidence: the online checkout landed with the ecommerce lead. The unified commerce slide: *"that's a diagram."* (quote, Bev, head of store operations). She then asked how a return works at the till, and the rep *"described it."*
- Insight: **the category claim failed exactly where it needed proof.**

**What decided it**
- Evidence: the store operations workshop. Six store-ops people against the rep and one solutions engineer, and nobody on our side had run a store rollout. Bev asked who does the installs, heard "a partner," and stopped asking (fact). Before that, we were ahead, per Raj.
- Insight: lost at the store-operations level, on rollout credibility.

**Product or proof gaps**
- Evidence: a reference call. *"can I talk to them?"* (quote, Angela), not arranged in time. A rollout plan. A live return of an online order at a store till: described, never shown.
- Insight: every gap was a **proof gap about stores**.

**Customer language**
- Evidence: *"One system for the tills and the website."* (Bev) · *"I don't want two vendors pointing at each other."* (Angela) · *"one customer, one basket."* · "the tills" vs. our "in-person payments" / "point of sale" · never "unified commerce."
- Insight: they describe outcomes. We describe categories.

## Close
- **Not covered:**
  - Adyen's commercial terms.
  - What Bev wrote down.
  - Whether a named rollout plan with partner installs would have been accepted.
- **Follow-ups:** ask Raj for a debrief and the list of 14 questions.
- **Rep takeaway:** show, don't describe, and sell to the person who owns the problem. "Better product online" didn't matter to the store-side decider.
