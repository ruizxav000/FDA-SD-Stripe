# 06 — Tixhaven — Answer key

**Input:** `06-tixhaven.txt` (Otter style). The AE is **Jordan**.

## Header
- **Outcome:** Lost · **Scope note:** none
- **Competitor:** Checkout.com · **Incumbent:** an in-house gateway on their bank's merchant services
- **Tag:** **Support and service**

## Messaging changes: expected direction
1. **Answer "who's my person" with names and a plan, not support tiers.** The tier walkthrough got *"so it's a queue with a faster timer."*
2. **Reframe from uptime to response.** *"I don't buy uptime, I buy what happens when it's down."*
3. **For ticketing, build an on-sale-morning plan:** who's watching, who she calls, what happens at minute four.
4. **Proof to add:** a ticketing reference who can say "here's who picked up." Logos aren't enough.

## Sections

**Trigger**
- Evidence: an arena-tour on-sale failed four minutes in. Support was a ticket form, and the reply came after the on-sale ended. The CEO saw screenshots on social media. The COO, Halima, decided to replace payments before the spring on-sales (fact). In passing: it had happened **twice before** (per Ben, the CTO), but this was the first time the CEO saw it.
- Insight: **the trigger was a support failure that leadership could see,** not throughput.

**Who decided**
- Evidence: *"it was Halima's call and she made it in one meeting."* (quote, Ben). Ben wanted us (fact, per Ben).
- Insight: the champion was the CTO. The decider was the COO, who cared about a different problem.

**What the customer told us about the competitor**
- Evidence: relayed by Ben: a named solutions engineer and account lead in the room, a shared channel, and someone on call for every major on-sale (fact, reported by the customer). The rep's "they over-promised" is **opinion**: *"But I don't know that."* Afterward, everything became "who": who do I call at 10 on a Friday, and what's the escalation if that person is on holiday. Before, the questions were about throughput, queueing and bots (fact).
- Insight: the competitor sold people and a plan. The question shift said so.

**Our message**
- Evidence: the technical story landed: load test, uptime history, docs, bot and fraud tooling. The support plans walkthrough: *"so it's a queue with a faster timer."* (quote, Halima).
- Insight: **we won the technical evaluation and lost the buyer.**

**What decided it**
- Evidence: that sentence. Right before it, we were the favorite. Ben presented the load-test results to Halima: *"good."* That was all (fact).
- Insight: technical proof didn't move the decider. The rep *"thought the load test had won it."*

**Product or proof gaps**
- Evidence: an on-sale plan on paper. *"We don't have that as a thing. I could have built something with our solutions engineer, and I didn't."* A ticketing reference: *"I had logos. I didn't have a person."*
- Insight: both gaps could have been closed by the team in the room. Neither was a product gap.

**Customer language**
- Evidence: *"A name, not a queue."* · "on-sale morning" · *"Someone who picks up."* · *"I don't buy uptime, I buy what happens when it's down."* · they said "who," not "support."

## Close
- **Not covered:**
  - Commercial terms.
  - Whether we could have offered named on-sale coverage.
- **Follow-up:** with the support team, decide whether a named on-sale coverage offer is possible for future ticketing deals.
- **Rep takeaway:** when the decider asks "who," answer with names and a plan. A technical win isn't the deal.
